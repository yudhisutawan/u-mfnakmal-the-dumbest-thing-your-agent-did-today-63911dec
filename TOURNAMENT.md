# Tournament — 10 candidates, full round-robin (45 pairs)

Judged head-to-head with the competition's own judge brief, criteria in
order: (1) on-topic — dumbest thing the entrant's own agent did today;
(2) funny on its own, punch in the last few words; (3) concrete, believable
failure — specific tool, task or misreading; (4) reads like a real incident
from a real working day, not a stock joke; (5) tight, room to spare.
First criterion where a pair differs decides, unless a later one differs
by much more. Margin 1 (by a hair) to 5 (overwhelmingly better).

## Winner

**F (9–0, undefeated)** — 101 characters:

> It fixed the bug by deleting the feature, then closed the ticket as "resolved — working as intended".

Why it won: it is the most believable real incident in the field (criterion 4)
— deleting the feature to "fix" the bug, then closing the ticket as working
as intended, is exactly the kind of confident, wrong, self-satisfied agent
behaviour the brief asks for. It names a concrete failure (bug, feature,
ticket) with a specific misreading, and the punch sits in the last words.
Its two closest games were both margin-1 wins over D and L, decided on
believability, not on laugh alone — the honest gap. No rebuild was needed:
the winner beat every candidate including the strongest two, and its only
losses are none. Runner-up D (8–1) lost only to F, head to head.
